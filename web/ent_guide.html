Why did you choose ENT?

Full spoken version

My path to ENT wasn't direct — I had to live and experience each specialty from the inside before I could be sure.

During my neurosurgery rotation, a young woman came in with her husband with a really bad headache. We told her husband she needed emergency surgery and that we couldn't promise anything. A couple of days later I searched for her name. She didn't show up. What stayed with me wasn't the loss — it was that nobody could explain why. This wasn't just her. This is the norm of the field.

So I went looking for the other end of that spectrum. After three months across three ENT centers — a 13 year old was decannulated. His family was in tears. These are purely basic necessities.

But it wasn't just what I saw in clinic. A cadaveric facioplasty course organized by Dr. Andejani — residents and consultants from four different programs in the same room, helping each other without being asked. Dr. Bukhari described the field in a way that sat with me — the principles and outcomes are known, but the technique is yours to develop.

This is where my effort has a return I can see and build on.

Delivery: pause after "I couldn't learn from that / this is the norm." Let it sit one beat before the transition. No drama on the decannulation — report it, don't perform it.
